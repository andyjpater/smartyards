# 🧭 THE SELL HEAPS MORE FRAMEWORK
# INTRIGUE → PAIN → EMPATHY → ANTIDOTE → ACTION

Every sale, from a $300 service call to a $300k build, moves through the same five stages. Skip one and the deal stalls. Most lost quotes can be traced back to a skipped stage, usually **Pain**.

**🎣 INTRIGUE** — Create enough curiosity to earn the next conversation.
**🩹 PAIN** — Understand what the customer actually wants solved.
**🤝 EMPATHY** — Show you understand the problem *and* what it’s costing them.
**💊 ANTIDOTE** — Position your product/service as the logical solution.
**✅ ACTION** — Help them make a decision and lock in the next step.

Below: each stage broken down, with trade and service examples. Read it once, then use it to diagnose every deal you post in {#helpMeCloseThis}.

## 🎣 1. INTRIGUE
**Job:** earn the next conversation. Not the sale — the *next step*.

Most tradies open with who they are and how long they’ve been around. Nobody cares yet. Open with something about *them*.

**Weak:** “Hi, it’s Dave from Dave’s Plumbing, we’ve been in business 20 years…”
**Strong:** “Hi Karen, it’s Dave from Dave’s Plumbing. You mentioned the hot water’s running out halfway through a shower. Is that every morning or just when everyone’s home?”

**Where Intrigue shows up:**
• First reply to a web enquiry (speed matters more than polish)
• The first 20 seconds of a cold call
• Subject line of a quote email
• The opening of a site visit

**Test:** did they lean in and start talking? If they’re giving one-word answers, you haven’t earned it yet.
<!-- split -->
## 🩹 2. PAIN
**Job:** find out what they actually want solved, and why now.

The customer asks for *a thing* (“quote for a new split system”). The sale is in the *reason* (“our daughter’s room hits 35 degrees and she can’t sleep before exams”).

**Questions that dig for pain:**
• “What’s got you looking at this now?”
• “What happens if you don’t get it sorted?”
• “What have you tried already?”
• “What’s the most annoying part of it day to day?”
• “If this was fixed perfectly, what would be different?”
• “Who else is affected by it?”

**Keep going one layer deeper.** The first answer is the polite one. The second or third is the real one.

⚠️ **Most common mistake in the whole Society:** jumping to the quote before finding the pain. Then you’re just a price on a page, and the cheapest price wins.
<!-- split -->
## 🤝 3. EMPATHY
**Job:** prove you understand the problem *and its consequences*, before you pitch anything.

This isn’t “I hear you” and a sad face. It’s playing their problem back to them better than they said it, so they think *“this person gets it.”*

**Example — electrician, switchboard upgrade:**
> “So the power trips a couple of times a week, it’s killed the fridge once already, and with the baby coming you’re worried about it happening at 2am. And the last sparky just reset it and left. That’s frustrating — you paid someone and the problem’s still there.”

**Why it works:** once someone feels understood, they stop comparing you on price and start trusting you on judgement.

**Empathy checklist:**
☐ Repeat the problem in their words
☐ Name the consequence (cost, stress, time, safety, family)
☐ Acknowledge what they’ve already tried
☐ Get a “yeah, exactly” before moving on
<!-- split -->
## 💊 4. ANTIDOTE
**Job:** position what you do as the *logical* fix for the pain you just uncovered.

Now, and only now, you talk about your solution. Connect every feature straight back to their pain. If it doesn’t link to something they told you, leave it out.

**Weak (feature dump):** “We use premium Italian porcelain, 10-year waterproofing warranty, fully licensed and insured…”
**Strong (antidote):** “You said the last bathroom leaked into the kitchen ceiling within two years. That’s exactly why we membrane the whole floor, not just the shower, and why we give a 10-year waterproofing warranty. That problem doesn’t happen again.”

**Options help:** give 2–3 options (good / better / best) linked to what they told you matters. It changes the question from *“yes or no?”* to *“which one?”* and lifts average sale.
<!-- split -->
## ✅ 5. ACTION
**Job:** help them decide, and lock in a specific next step. Every interaction ends with one.

The customer usually wants to decide. They just don’t want to be pushed. Your job is to make the next step clear and easy.

**Closing questions that don’t feel like closing:**
• “Which of the three options feels right for you?”
• “What would you need to see to go ahead?”
• “We’ve got a start date on the 14th. Want me to pencil you in?”
• “Is there anything stopping us from getting this booked today?”

**Never end on:** “Have a think and let me know.”
**End on:** “I’ll call you Thursday at 5 once you’ve spoken to Mark. Does that suit?” — then **call Thursday at 5**.

No next step = no deal. It just hasn’t died yet.

## 🔍 USING THE FRAMEWORK TO DIAGNOSE DEALS
When a deal stalls, find the stage that got skipped:

• **No reply to your first message** → Intrigue problem
• **“You’re too expensive” / “got a cheaper quote”** → Pain or Empathy problem (they don’t see the difference)
• **“Just email me the quote”** → Intrigue or Pain problem (you haven’t earned the conversation)
• **“Need to think about it”** → Antidote problem (not convinced it fixes their thing) or Action problem (no next step set)
• **Ghosted after the quote** → Action problem

When you post in {#helpMeCloseThis}, {#objectionClinic} or {#lostDeals}, try naming which stage you think broke. You’ll start seeing it everywhere.
