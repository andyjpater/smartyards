# “You’re too expensive.”
tags: Price
**What it usually means:** “I don’t see why you cost more,” or “that’s more than I expected.”
**Don’t:** drop your price or defend it straight away.

> “Fair enough, it’s not a small investment. Can I ask, too expensive compared to what? Another quote, or what you had in mind?”

**If compared to budget:** “What were you hoping to spend? … Let’s look at what we could do around that number, or which part matters most to do now.”
**If compared to another quote:** see *“I’ve got a cheaper quote.”*

**Then:** re-anchor on their pain. “You said the main thing was [pain]. This is how we make sure that doesn’t happen again.”
# “I’ve got a cheaper quote.”
tags: Price, Competitor
**What it usually means:** they can’t see the difference, so they’re comparing on price.

> “Totally fair to compare. Can I ask, is it for the same scope? Often the difference is in what’s *not* included. Mind if we go through it side by side?”

**Things to compare:** materials, warranty, licensing/insurance, timeline, clean-up, who actually does the work, what happens if something goes wrong.

**Then:** “If both quotes were exactly the same price, who would you go with?” If they say you, ask what it would take to close the gap. If they say the other bloke, you’ve learned something important.
# “I need to think about it.”
tags: Stall
**What it usually means:** there’s an unanswered question or concern they haven’t said out loud.

> “Of course, it’s a big decision. Usually when people say that, there’s one part they’re not 100% sure on. Is it the price, the timing, or something about the job itself?”

Then listen. Deal with the real thing.

**If it really is just time:** “No worries. When do you think you’ll have it sorted? I’ll give you a call Thursday arvo. Does that work?” Then **call Thursday arvo**.
# “I need to talk to my partner.”
tags: Decision maker
**What it usually means:** exactly that, or a polite exit. Either way, you want to be in the conversation.

> “Makes sense. It’s their house too. What do you think they’ll want to know? I can make sure you’ve got the answers.”

**Better:** “Would it be easier if I jumped on a quick call with both of you? Takes 10 minutes and saves you having to explain all the options.”

**Prevent it next time:** in qualifying, ask “Who else is involved in the decision? Can they be there when I come out?”
# “Just email me the quote.”
tags: Brush-off
**What it usually means:** “I want to compare prices without talking to you.”

> “Absolutely, I’ll send it through. The quotes we do usually have a couple of options, and they make a lot more sense with a 10-minute walkthrough. Otherwise you’re decoding it on your own. Can we book in a quick call tomorrow once you’ve got it?”

**If they insist:** send it, and in the same message: “I’ll give you a quick call Wednesday to answer any questions.” Then call Wednesday.
# “We’re getting three quotes.”
tags: Competitor
**What it usually means:** normal, sensible behaviour. Don’t fight it. Help them compare properly.

> “Smart move. What’s most important to you when you’re comparing them? Price, quality, timing, someone who turns up when they say?”

Then make sure your quote clearly shows how you deliver on *their* answer.

**Bonus:** “When the quotes come in, happy to go through them with you so you’re comparing apples with apples, even if you don’t go with us.” Confident, helpful, and you often win it.
# “Not interested.”
tags: Brush-off
**Usually on cold calls.** Don’t argue. Get curious.

> “No worries at all. Just so I don’t bother you again, is that because you’ve already got someone for [work], or it’s just not a priority right now?”

**Got someone:** “Good to hear. If they ever let you down, can I flick you my details so you’ve got a backup?”
**Not a priority:** “Fair enough. Mind if I check back in [3 months]?”

Always leave the door open. Plenty of “not interested” turn into jobs six months later.
# “We’ll call you.”
tags: Stall
**What it usually means:** “We probably won’t call you.”

> “Great. What would need to happen for you to be ready to go ahead?”

Then book the follow-up yourself:
> “How about I give you a buzz next Tuesday? If you’re not ready, no drama, just tell me.”

**Rule:** never hand the next step to the customer. You own the follow-up.
# “Can you do it cheaper?”
tags: Price
**What it usually means:** they want the job, and they’re testing whether your price is real.

**Never** just drop the price. That tells them your first price was padded.

> “I can’t do the same job for less and do it properly. What I can do is look at the scope. Which parts are most important to you?”

**Trade, don’t give:** less scope, different materials, flexible timing (fill a gap in your schedule), or payment upfront, in exchange for a change in price.

> “If you’re flexible on the start date, I can fit you in at the end of the month for $X.”
