# Cold call opener — commercial / B2B
tags: Cold call
**Use for:** calling property managers, builders, strata, facilities managers.

> “Hi [Name], it’s [You] from [Business]. I’ll be quick, I know I’m calling out of the blue. We look after [type of work] for [similar customer] around [area]. Most [property managers] we talk to are sick of [common pain — trades not showing up / slow quotes / callbacks]. Is that something you deal with, or have you got it sorted?”

**If yes:** “What usually happens?” → dig for pain.
**If sorted:** “Good to hear. Who do you use at the moment? … If they ever let you down, can I send through my details so you’ve got a backup?”

**Goal:** a meeting or permission to quote, not a sale.
# Lead response — first call to a new enquiry
tags: Lead response
**Use for:** calling a web / Facebook / hipages enquiry. Call within **5 minutes** if you can. Speed beats polish.

> “Hi [Name], it’s [You] from [Business]. You just sent through an enquiry about [job]. Have I caught you at an OK time?
>
> Great. Tell me a bit about what’s going on with the [job]?”

Then shut up and listen. Ask 2–3 pain questions (see *Qualification questions*).

**Close the call with a next step:**
> “Sounds like the best thing is for me to come out and have a look. I’ve got Tuesday at 7:30am or Wednesday at 3. Which is better?”
# Lead response — SMS when they don’t answer
tags: Lead response
**Send straight after a missed first call.**

> Hi [Name], [You] from [Business] here. Just tried calling about your [job] enquiry. When’s a good time for a quick 5 min chat? Or reply here if easier 👍

**No reply in 3 hours:**
> Hi [Name], still keen to help with the [job]. Quick question so I can point you the right way: is this something you’re hoping to get done in the next month or so, or further out?

A simple question gets far more replies than “let me know if you have any questions”.
# Qualification questions
tags: Qualifying
**Ask before you book a site visit or write a quote.** A 2-hour visit to a tyre-kicker is expensive.

**The job**
• What’s got you looking at this now?
• What happens if it doesn’t get done?

**Timing**
• When are you hoping to have this finished by? What’s driving that date?

**Budget** (ask it, don’t guess it)
• “Jobs like this usually land between $X and $Y depending on [factors]. Is that roughly what you had in mind?”

**Decision**
• Who else is involved in the decision?
• Will they be there when I come out?

**Competition**
• Are you getting other quotes? What’s important to you when choosing who does it?

If they won’t answer budget or decision questions, they’re probably not serious yet.
# Site visit structure
tags: Site visit
**The 5-part site visit. 30–60 minutes for most jobs.**

**1. Open (2 min)** — On time. Shoes off if needed. “Before I start measuring, can you walk me through what’s going on and what you’d like it to look like?”

**2. Pain (10 min)** — Ask, don’t tell. Why now? What’s annoying? What have they tried? What does perfect look like?

**3. Inspect (10–30 min)** — Do the measure/look. Talk through what you find: “See this here? This is why it keeps happening.”

**4. Play back + options (5 min)** — Empathy, then antidote. “So what you need is… The way I’d approach it is… There are a couple of ways we could do it…”

**5. Next step (2 min)** — “I’ll have the quote to you by Thursday. Can we book 15 minutes Thursday evening to go through it together so you’re not decoding it on your own?”

**Never leave without a booked quote walkthrough.**
# Quote presentation script
tags: Quote presentation
**Present the quote. Never just email it and pray.** Walk through on a call, video or in person.

> “Before we get into the numbers, let me recap what you told me matters: [pain 1], [pain 2], [pain 3]. Did I get that right?
>
> I’ve put together three options.
> **Option 1** fixes [core problem] — $X.
> **Option 2** fixes that plus [pain 2] — $Y. This is what most people in your situation go with.
> **Option 3** is the full job, [everything] — $Z.
>
> Which one feels closest to what you’re after?”

Then **stop talking**. First person to speak after the price usually loses.

If they hesitate: “What’s going through your mind?”
# Follow-up SMS — after quote sent
tags: Follow-up
**24 hours after the quote:**
> Hi [Name], it’s [You]. Just checking the quote came through OK and made sense. Any questions I can answer? Happy to jump on a quick call.

**3–4 days after:**
> Hi [Name], had a thought about your [job]. [One useful point — e.g. “If you go with option 2, we could do the tiling in the same week and save you a second trip.”] Worth a 5 min chat?

**7–10 days after:**
> Hi [Name], I’m finalising our schedule for [month]. Do you want me to hold a spot for your [job], or should I let it go for now? Either answer is totally fine.
# Follow-up email — quote gone quiet
tags: Follow-up
**Subject:** Your [job] — quick question

> Hi [Name],
>
> I haven’t heard back on the [job] quote, so I’m guessing one of three things has happened:
>
> 1. You’re still keen but life got busy
> 2. Something in the quote didn’t sit right
> 3. You’ve gone another way
>
> Any of those is fine. Could you just reply with 1, 2 or 3 so I know where we stand?
>
> Cheers,
> [You]

Stupidly effective. People will reply to a number when they won’t reply to “just following up”.
# Closing questions
tags: Closing
**Use when you’ve covered their pain and presented options. Pick one, ask it, then shut up.**

• “Which option feels right for you?”
• “What would you need to see to go ahead today?”
• “Is there anything stopping us from getting this booked in?”
• “We’ve got a start on the [date]. Want me to lock that in for you?”
• “On a scale of 1–10, how ready are you to go ahead? … What would make it a 10?”
• “If we can [solve the last concern], are you happy to go ahead?”

**Assumptive close** (when the signals are clear):
• “Great. I’ll send the deposit invoice through now and get you booked for the 14th.”
# Reactivation — old quotes & past customers
tags: Reactivation
**Old quote (3–12 months):**
> Hi [Name], [You] from [Business] here. We quoted your [job] back in [month]. Just wondering if that ever got sorted? If not, we’ve got some availability in [month] and I’d be happy to update the quote.

**Past customer:**
> Hi [Name], [You] from [Business]. We did your [job] back in [year]. Hope it’s all still going well! We’re booking in [season] work now. Anything else around the place you’ve been meaning to get done?

**Send in batches of 20–50, then call everyone who replies.** An old database is the cheapest lead source you’ll ever have.
# Referral request
tags: Referrals & reviews
**Ask at the moment of highest happiness: job handover, when they’re saying thanks.**

> “Really glad you’re happy with it. Can I ask a favour? Most of our work comes from people like you telling friends. Is there anyone you know who’s been talking about getting [similar work] done?”

**By SMS, a week after the job:**
> Hi [Name], hope you’re enjoying the new [job]! If you know anyone else who needs a hand with [work], I’d really appreciate you passing on my number. I’ll look after them like I looked after you.
# Review request
tags: Referrals & reviews
**Send same day or next day, while they’re still excited.** Make it one tap.

> Hi [Name], thanks again for having us. Hope you love the [job]! Would you mind leaving us a quick Google review? It makes a massive difference for a small business like ours. [link] Thanks heaps!

**In person first:** “If you’re happy with it, would you mind if I sent you a link for a quick Google review?” People who say yes in person are much more likely to do it.
